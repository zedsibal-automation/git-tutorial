A flower that teaches us to pause

In Japan, cherry blossoms symbolize the beauty of fleeting moments. The flowers bloom for only about one to two weeks, which is why people gather every spring to enjoy them. This custom is called hanami, meaning “flower viewing.” The idea behind it, mono no aware, is a gentle sadness that makes beauty feel more precious.

  • 1–2 weeks Typical full-bloom period
  • 5 petals On most wild cherry blossoms
  • 1912 Year Japan gifted trees to Washington, D.C.

Varieties

Somei Yoshino

The most common type. Pale pink-white blossoms that cover bare branches before the leaves appear.

Shidarezakura

The weeping cherry. Branches drape downward like a pink waterfall.

Yaezakura

Fluffy, double-petaled blooms that open later in the season.

Kawazu-zakura

An early bloomer with deeper pink petals, flowering as early as February.

Places to see them

  • Philosopher’s Path, Kyoto

    A canal-side walk lined with trees.

  • Mount Yoshino, Nara

    A mountain covered in tens of thousands of trees, blooming in waves from bottom to top.

  • Hirosaki Castle, Aomori

    Famous for blossoms framing the castle moat.

  • Tidal Basin, Washington, D.C.

    Home of the National Cherry Blossom Festival.

Bloom calendar

  1. Budding

    Small buds swell on bare branches.

  2. First bloom

    The first few flowers open.

  3. Full bloom (mankai)

    The peak, with branches covered in flowers.

  4. Hanafubuki

    Petals fall like a pink snowstorm.

Timing varies by location. In Japan, blooms start in the warm south and move north over several weeks, so always check a current forecast before traveling.

Hanami tips

  • Arrive early to claim a good spot
  • Bring a picnic blanket and snacks
  • Visit on a weekday to avoid crowds
  • Take photos in soft morning light
  • Leave no trash behind
