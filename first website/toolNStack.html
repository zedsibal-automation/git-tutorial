The Stack Behind the Builds

The tools I actually use on real projects, what they're good at, and where they let me down.

I only write about tools I've used on real client work, including the ones I stopped using. No tool is perfect, so each one below comes with an honest note on what it does well and what to watch out for.

Automation Platforms

n8n

My default for complex builds. It handles branching logic, custom code, and error handling well, and you can self-host it if a client needs control over their data.

Watch out for: It has a steeper learning curve, and non-technical clients can find it intimidating to maintain.

Read my n8n posts →

Make

Great when the client needs to understand it. The visual layout is easy to follow, which makes handoff and training simpler.

Watch out for: Large scenarios can get messy, and costs can climb as your operation count grows.

Read my Make posts →

Zapier

The fastest way to connect two apps. It has a huge app library and a gentle learning curve, so it's ideal for simple automations.

Watch out for: It gets expensive and awkward once workflows need heavy logic or high volume.

Read my Zapier posts →

AI Models

OpenAI

Reliable for structured tasks. I use it for classification, extraction, and short drafting steps inside workflows.

Watch out for: It can state wrong answers confidently, so any step that talks to customers needs guardrails and testing.

Claude

Strong for writing and longer context. I reach for it when a step needs careful tone, such as follow-up emails, or when it has to read long documents.

Watch out for: Like any model, it needs clear instructions and testing on real inputs before it touches a customer.

Data and Business Apps

Airtable and Google Sheets

My go-to places for logs, lookups, and simple dashboards. Every workflow I build writes its runs somewhere I can review, which makes debugging far easier.

Stripe, Slack, and Gmail

The apps where the real work happens: payments come in, alerts land in Slack, and emails go out through Gmail. Most of my builds connect at least one of them.

How I Choose a Tool

  1. Who will maintain it? If a non-technical team owns it, I favor the simpler tool.
  2. How complex is the logic? Branching and custom code point toward n8n.
  3. What does it cost at scale? I estimate volume before I commit to a platform.
  4. How does it fail? I want clear error messages and an easy way to retry.
